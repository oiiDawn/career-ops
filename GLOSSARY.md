# Career Ops

Career Ops evaluates job opportunities using evidence relevant to the role and the candidate's preferences.

## Language

**Company research**:
Research into an employer's business condition, working culture, and compensation as they apply to a particular opportunity. These three research dimensions are distinct from the opportunity's career direction.

**Applicable evidence**:
Source material that supports a claim about the relevant employer, role, location, level, team, and time period. Material with a different or uncertain scope must retain that limitation.

**Research finding**:
A sourced statement that distinguishes supported facts, inferences, and unresolved questions. An unsupported inference is not a verified fact.

**Decision reference**:
Relevant sourced information that can inform screening even when applicability to the exact role is uncertain. Same-employer group policies, other-team experiences and salary reference levels retain their scope and limitations; they are not confirmed job conditions.

**Evidence sufficiency**:
Whether the available evidence supports a bounded screening reference in the declared dimension. It does not measure completeness of internal company information or verification of a future offer.

**Research quality**:
The correctness, applicability, and coverage of findings, including their treatment of uncertainty. Fluency and agreement with an expected score do not establish research quality.

**Research summary**:
A concise account of findings that preserves their source references and material limitations. Summarizing must not turn uncertainty into certainty.

**Assessment record**:
The structured result of scoring one opportunity: dimension scores, research findings, recommendation, and the provenance of every input. It is the only authority for anything a program checks or reads.

**Score report**:
The account of one assessment record written for the candidate to read, supporting the decision whether to apply and what to confirm first. It is a one-way projection of the assessment record; nothing is read back out of it, and it carries no provenance hashes. Interview preparation is not part of it.

**Capability match**:
The verdict on whether the candidate's evidence meets one requirement a posting lists: proven, adjacent, gap, or unverified. Every listed requirement is judged once, and that single judgment is used everywhere. It describes the chance of getting the role, not the role's attractiveness, so it gates which opportunities are assessed and is shown to the candidate, but is never scored as a dimension.

**Provenance hash**:
A content hash that identifies the exact input or output an assessment was built from or published as. It belongs to the assessment record, never to report prose. Distinct from a discovery fingerprint, which identifies duplicate postings.
_Avoid_: fingerprint (when a content hash is meant)

**Candidate preference**:
A personal priority or threshold used to score an opportunity's attractiveness. Preferences affect scoring, not the collection or description of public employer facts.
